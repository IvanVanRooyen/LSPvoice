# LSPC15mf 15.6-inch bedside terminal (orange button at the bottom centre)

This guide applies only when the caller confirms that their bedside screen has an **orange button in the middle of the frame, just below the screen**. The LSPC15mf is the only LSPcare terminal with that button, so it identifies the model. The hospital bed does not need to be known first.

To check, ask: "Is there an orange button in the middle of the frame, just below the screen?" If the caller says no, isn't sure, or describes something different (for example a row of buttons along the bottom edge, a light or lamp button, or a telephone handset), this guide does not apply. Use the unknown-hardware guidance instead and do not use any step below.

## What this terminal has, and does not have

- **No status or indicator light.** Never ask the caller to check a light on this terminal.
- **Orange button (bottom centre):** turns the screen off or on. That is all it does. It is not a Home button and it cannot call a nurse, the helpdesk or anyone else.
- **Side button:** a short press turns the screen off or on. Pressing and holding it turns the whole terminal off. Never ask the caller to press and hold the side button.
- **Volume buttons:** volume + and − buttons are on the side edge of the frame, and patients may use them.
- **Headphones:** the terminal has a 3.5 mm headphone socket on the side, and it also supports Bluetooth headphones. Headphones are not supplied by LSP or the hospital, so the patient needs their own.
- **Connecting Bluetooth headphones:** patients can do this themselves. On the home screen, open **APPS**, then the **Bluetooth** icon (on some screens it is inside Settings). Make sure the Bluetooth switch is turned on, then select **Scan & clear paired devices** at the top right and follow the steps on the screen. If the caller can't find these icons, don't guess. Offer the support process.
- **Not fitted:** no remote control, no telephone handset, no card-tap (NFC) reader and no nurse-call button on the terminal. For nursing help, the caller uses the hospital's own nurse-call system or asks ward staff.

## Black screen

A short press of either button only switches the screen off, so a black screen may simply have been switched off by a bump or a press.

1. If the caller needs a nurse or any clinical help, use the nurse-call/ward-staff redirect first and stop here.
2. Ask: "Is the screen completely black, or can you see anything on it?" If something is showing, this step does not apply. Help with what they can see.
3. If it is completely black and the caller can reach the screen comfortably, say: "Please press the orange button once. It's in the middle of the frame, just below the screen." Ask them to wait a few seconds.
4. If the screen comes back on, the problem is solved.
5. If it stays black, do not ask them to press it again repeatedly, hold the side button, unplug anything, move the arm or check a light. Offer the support process.

Patients can normally reach the buttons from the bed. If this caller cannot reach the screen comfortably, do not ask them to stretch or move. Offer the support process.
