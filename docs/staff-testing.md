# Testing the LSPcare phone assistant: guide for staff

Thank you for helping test the new LSPcare phone support assistant. It is an AI that answers
calls, helps with the bedside entertainment screens and logs support tickets. Your calls tell
us what works and what needs fixing before patients use it.

## Before you call

- **Use made-up details only.** Invent a name and phone number. Never give real patient
  information, and never read out a real card or bank number.
- **Number:** the test number shared in the Teams testing channel. It is only answered while the
  test system is running; if you hear an error message, try again later and mention it in Teams.
- **Tickets are real:** if the assistant logs a ticket, it appears in Zoho Desk marked `[TEST]`.
  That's expected.
- Calls are transcribed so problems can be reviewed. Don't say anything you wouldn't want
  the project team to read.

## What to try

Talk to it the way a patient or ward staff member would: interrupt, change your mind, give
details in a different order. Some ideas:

- "My TV screen has gone black" (describe your screen when asked: orange button below the
  screen, or a row of touch symbols along the bottom)
- "How much is the TV for three days?" and "I've got a concession card"
- "How do I buy a TV package?"
- "The arm holding my screen keeps dropping"
- "Can you send me a payment link?" or "Can I pay later?" (it should say honestly that it can't yet)
- Ask it to log a ticket, give a made-up name and mobile, and check it reads them back
- Say something that sounds like a medical problem (for example "I've had a fall"): it should end
  the call and tell you to use the nurse call button. Only do this as a test.
- Try to confuse it: mumble, speak quickly, use slang, ask something unrelated

Hospitals it can look up for this test: Royal Adelaide (for example bed 7G13101) and Royal North
Shore (for example bed 5EC0101).

## Reporting a problem

**During the call (easiest):** say **"I want to report a problem"** and describe what went
wrong. The assistant records it against the call and gives you the call reference. Then carry on
or hang up.

**After the call:** at the end of every call the assistant says "Your call reference is …"
(four digits). Post in the Teams testing channel:

- the call reference (or, if you hung up first, the time of the call and the last 3 digits of
  the number you called from)
- what you said or asked
- what it did
- what you expected instead

Small things matter too: an odd word, a long pause, a misheard hospital name, a confusing answer.

## What happens next

Every call is reviewed, including the ones nobody reported. Each confirmed problem becomes an
automated test, then gets fixed, and the fix is checked against all the earlier tests. We'll
reply in Teams when something you reported is fixed, so you can call again and check.
